# T-007 performance spike – run 1 (2026-11-17)

**Device:** MacBook Air M5 (DM's reference notebook), on power · **Display:** Samsung The Frame 4K TV, connected via HDMI
**Build:** `pnpm spike:desktop` at `82ab15d` (Tauri 2.12 / WKWebView, PixiJS 8.22 WebGL) · **Video:** synthetic 3840×2160, 30 fps, H.264 ~40 Mbit/s

## Analysis

1. **No 4K rendering was measured.** macOS drove the TV at 1920×1080 with devicePixelRatio 1, so "native" and "Full HD" rendered the same 1920×1080 pixels (the spike warned about it). The TV upscaled the 1080p signal itself.
2. **The video alone already misses the budget; fog and tokens cost a further ~13 % FPS.** Video only: 53.6 FPS with 46.6 % missed refreshes. Full scene: 46.5 / 45.8 FPS (average interval 18.6 → 21.5 ms), although its missed share is lower. No degradation in the repeat, so no sign of thermal throttling within the run.
3. **The frame-time distribution suggests long frames followed by quick catch-up frames.** In "video only", 46.6 % of intervals exceed 25.5 ms, yet the mean interval is 18.6 ms and p50 is 16 ms. That only fits if the remaining intervals average well below one refresh (≤ ~11 ms even in the most favourable case). The exact length of the long frames is not in the data (p95 44 ms, p99 48 ms).
4. **Hypothesis (not profiled): the per-frame upload of the 4K video into a WebGL texture blocks the main thread.** It fits points 2–3: with a 30 fps video roughly every second display frame receives a new video frame, PixiJS uploads each decoded frame (~33 MB RGBA at 4K) independently of the render resolution, and fog/tokens add comparatively little. The "0 dropped video frames" cannot be used as evidence that decoding keeps up – WebKit may not report these counters for file playback. Run 2 tests the hypothesis directly.
5. **The distribution varies a lot between identical setups.** "Full HD" and "native" rendered the same pixels, yet p50 is 29 vs 15–16 ms and the missed share 62 % vs 35–37 % at similar average FPS – possibly a beat between the 30 fps video and the ~59 Hz display. Run 2 is therefore judged on clear differences in average FPS together with the missed share, not on p50 alone or single-digit percentage gaps.
6. The 20 s video looped once inside each 25 s window; the 102 ms maximum may be that loop restart.

**Interim conclusion:** with the video uploaded as a WebGL texture, the budget is clearly missed – even at 1080p output. Because the suspected cost sits in one step with several alternatives, run 2 measures those alternatives before concluding on ADR-0002.
