# T-007 performance spike – runs 2 and 3, conclusion (2026-11-17)

**Device:** MacBook Air M5 (DM's reference notebook), on power · **Display:** Samsung The Frame 4K TV
**Build:** `pnpm spike:desktop` at `d90f115` (Tauri 2.12 / WKWebView, PixiJS 8.22) · every scenario runs in a freshly loaded page
**Videos:** synthetic 40 s, 30 fps, H.264 – 3840×2160 (~40 Mbit/s) and 1920×1080 (~12 Mbit/s) · 20 tokens · fog with blur rebuilt every frame (worst case)
**Method:** 5 s warm-up, 20 s measurement per scenario. A frame _misses a refresh_ when its interval exceeds 1.5 × the measured refresh interval. Video frame counters are only reported by WebKit for a video element in the page (scenarios E/F).

## Run 2 – 4K at 30 Hz

TV at 3840×2160 via an adapter limited to 30 Hz (refresh ≈ 30.3 Hz, devicePixelRatio 2). 60 FPS cannot be judged here, but the scenarios compare (missed = interval > 50 ms):

| --------------------------------------------- | ---- | ------------------------------------- |

| B full scene · WebGL texture · 4K video       | 29.9 | 36.5 %                                |
| C full scene · WebGL texture · 1080p video    | 30.0 | 0 %                                   |
| D full scene · WebGPU texture · 4K video      | 30.0 | 37.7 %                                |

The DM watched E and F on the TV: the video looked smooth.

## Analysis

1. **The run-1 hypothesis holds: uploading the 4K video into a texture is the bottleneck.** Every scenario that uploads 4K video frames into a texture misses the budget (A, B, D, G, H), whatever the renderer (WebGL or WebGPU) and the render resolution (native or Full HD). A 1080p video (C) or no upload at all (E, F) removes the problem.
2. **WebGPU is available in the app on macOS but does not help.** D averages 59 FPS but misses 46 % of refreshes – uneven frame pacing, i.e. visible judder.
3. **A video element under a transparent PixiJS canvas reaches ~60 FPS at 4K.** E: 59.8 FPS, p95 19 ms, 1 dropped video frame in 20 s, with 4K video, 4K render, the full scene and the worst-case fog. The render resolution makes no measurable difference (E vs F), so the Full HD setting (T-116) is a fallback for weaker devices, not a requirement on the reference notebook.
4. **E misses 1.25 % of refreshes – just above the 1 % budget.** About 15 short hitches (≤ 35 ms) in 20 s. The spike rebuilds the blurred fog geometry every frame; in play, fog only changes when it is revealed. Whether realistic fog stays below 1 % has to be shown by the renderer benchmark (T-105).
5. **The connection matters.** With an adapter limited to 4K at 30 Hz (run 2) every scenario is capped at 30 FPS. 4K at 60 Hz needs an adapter/cable supporting HDMI 2.0 or newer.
6. Thermal: the repeats (H) are slightly worse than B in both runs – possible mild throttling of the fanless notebook; it does not change the conclusions.

## Conclusion

- **ADR-0002 (Tauri 2 + React + PixiJS) is confirmed – with one change:** animated maps are shown as an HTML video element underneath a transparent PixiJS canvas instead of as a texture ([ADR-0010](../adr/0010-video-maps-as-video-element.md), accepted by the DM).
- **Budget:** met in practice (59.8 FPS, p95 19 ms), formally missed by a hair in the worst-case scene (1.25 % vs ≤ 1 % missed refreshes). The budget is not relaxed; T-105 must demonstrate ≤ 1 % with realistic fog and investigate the remaining hitches.
- **Budget definition:** ARCHITECTURE §11 now defines the frame-rate budget by missed refreshes, because a literal "p95 ≤ 16.7 ms" is unreachable even at a perfect 60 Hz (timer rounding, interval jitter). Approved by the DM.
- **Hardware note for the table:** use an adapter/cable that carries 4K at 60 Hz.
